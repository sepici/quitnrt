# Privacy policy

**The Last Hit: Quit NRT**, for Android

Last updated: 24 October 2026

## In short

The Last Hit doesn't collect any data. There's no account, no ads, no analytics and no crash
reporting. The app has no internet access, so it can't send anything over the internet itself.
What you log stays on your phone unless you export it, your phone's own backup copies it (see
"Backups and moving to a new phone"), or you ask an assistant about it. The developer never
receives any of it.

## What the app keeps on your phone

To work, the app stores these on your phone, in its own private storage, which other apps can't
read:

- **Your products:** the NRT you use, with each one's strength and the daily limit from its pack.
- **Your doses:** for each one, the product, the amount, the time and its time zone, where it was
  logged from (such as the app, the widget, the tile, a link or an assistant), when it was logged
  or last changed, and anything you add: tags such as "After a meal", how strong the craving was,
  whether it was planned, and a note.
- **Your tags,** including any you've added or renamed.
- **Your plan:** the usual day it starts from, each week's mix, daily guide and allowance, and
  each week's result.
- **The game:** your demon's name, creature and colours, its HP, the hits you've earned and
  spent, and your badges and gear.
- **Breathing sessions** from the breathing timer.
- **Your settings:** your country (to show the products sold there), when you stopped smoking or
  vaping if you said, when your day starts, sound, and when you accepted the disclaimer.
- **Reminder settings,** such as quiet hours and which reminders are on. These stay on this phone
  and aren't part of any backup.

The app itself never sends any of it to the developer or to anyone else. It only leaves the
phone in the ways described below: a file you export, your phone's own backup, or an answer to an
assistant you ask.

## What the app can and can't do on your phone

The app asks for only these permissions:

| Permission | What it's for |
|---|---|
| Notifications | The reminders you switch on, the Quick Settings tile's note with Undo, and the delay timer's end. On Android 13 and newer, the app asks in the reminders step when you set it up, or, if you skip that, the first time you switch a reminder on in Settings. |
| Vibrate | The breathing and delay timers buzz. |
| Keep the phone awake | The delay timer buzzes at its end with the screen locked. Android's WorkManager, which runs the reminders, uses it too. |
| Run at start-up | So reminders are set again after the phone restarts. It comes with WorkManager. |
| Foreground service | WorkManager lists it. The app doesn't use it. |

It doesn't ask for internet access, location, contacts, the camera, the microphone, your files
or your accounts. Reading a log link from an NFC sticker needs no permission.

## Backups and moving to a new phone

**Files you make.** In Settings, under Backup, you can export a full backup (a .json file) or
your doses (a .csv file), and restore a backup later. Android's file picker asks where each file
goes. Once it's saved, the file is yours: if you put it in a cloud folder or send it to someone,
that service's terms, or that person, decide what happens to it. The app keeps no copy and sends
it nowhere.

**Android's own backup.** If backup is on in your phone's settings, Android adds the app's data
to your phone's backup in your Google account, except the reminder settings, so it can come back
on a new phone. Google runs that service under its own privacy policy, and the developer can't
see it. On Android 9 and newer with a screen lock set, Android encrypts this backup with your
screen lock. Without a screen lock, or on Android 8, it isn't encrypted that way, and Google holds
it in your account under its own terms. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings, under
Backup.

**Copying to a new phone.** If you copy your apps across when you set up a new phone, the same
data goes straight to the new phone.

## What shows outside the app

Some things show outside the app, on your phone only:

- **Notifications,** for the reminders you've switched on, the Quick Settings tile's note with
  Undo after it logs a dose, and the delay timer's end. Your phone's settings decide whether they
  show on the lock screen.
- **The home-screen widget,** if you add one, shows today's amount and your quick-log products.
- **Shortcuts,** when you long-press the app's icon, name up to 3 of your quick-log products.
- **The Quick Settings tile,** if you add it, logs your first quick-log product.

## Links and assistants

**Log links.** The app opens `lasthit://log` links, from Tasker, Routines or an NFC sticker, to
log a dose. They bring a dose in; nothing goes out. The app shows what each one logged, with Undo.

**Quitlines.** The advice pages list a quitline for your country. Its button opens the number in
your phone app or the page in your browser; nothing about you goes with them.

**Assistants on Android 16 and newer.** The app offers a few functions to assistants on your
phone, such as Gemini, through Android's AppFunctions: log a dose, list your products, give
today's and this week's totals with the demon's HP, undo the last dose, and suggest a piece of
advice. Only assistants Android trusts with this can call them, and they're meant to do so when
you ask. The app answers with what each request needs and nothing more. What the assistant does
with the answer is covered by the assistant's own privacy policy: Gemini, for one, may handle
your request online. Google hasn't opened this to every app yet, so it may not work with your
assistant.

## Deleting your data

- **One dose:** in History, swipe it to the left, and Undo brings it straight back if you need it.
  The app keeps a deleted dose hidden for 30 days, then removes it for good the next time it
  opens.
- **Everything:** in your phone's Settings, open Apps, then The Last Hit, then Storage, and clear
  its storage. Or uninstall the app. Either one removes everything the app keeps on the phone.
- **Files you exported:** delete them wherever you saved them.
- **Android's backup:** manage it in your phone's backup settings or your Google account.

Since the developer never receives your data, the developer holds nothing about you to ask for,
change or delete. What the app keeps is on your phone, and in any file or backup described above.

## Children

The app is made for adults who use NRT. It collects nothing from anyone, children included.

## Not medical advice

The Last Hit helps you keep track and plan. It isn't medical advice and it isn't a medical
device. Talk to a pharmacist or GP before you change how you use NRT, especially if you're
pregnant, breastfeeding or living with a heart condition. Using NRT for longer is far safer than
smoking.

## Changes to this policy

If the way the app handles data ever changes, this page changes first, with a new date at the
top, and the update's notes in Google Play say so. The app has no internet access, so it can't
tell you itself.
